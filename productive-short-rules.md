\# 高产的短规则



我越来越感觉，我们世界的基本规则在相同描述复杂度下可能异常“高产”。基本物理的描述极短：经典场论只需要很少的微分和几何结构，量子力学和量子场论的基本框架也同样简洁。



更惊人的是，丰富性很早就出现了。非相对论量子力学、Coulomb 相互作用和费米统计这样一个很小的有效规则集，就已经足以产生原子、分子、晶体、复杂材料，以及进一步的生命和大脑。



这让人自然地问：在描述复杂度相近的可能世界中，我们的世界是否接近某种“结构生成能力”的极值——用极少的规则，产生极多层次的稳定结构？



\# Productive Short Rules



I increasingly suspect that, among worlds with fundamental laws of comparable description complexity, ours may be unusually productive. Fundamental physics is described by remarkably short rules: classical field theories use only a small amount of differential and geometric structure, while the basic frameworks of quantum mechanics and quantum field theory are similarly concise.



More strikingly, richness appears very early. A small effective rule set—nonrelativistic quantum mechanics, Coulomb interactions, and fermionic statistics—is already enough to produce atoms, molecules, crystals, complex materials, and eventually life and brains.



This suggests a natural question: among possible worlds of comparable description complexity, might ours lie near an extreme of structural productivity—generating exceptionally many stable levels of organization from exceptionally short rules?
